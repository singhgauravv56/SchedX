How the Smart Timetable Engine Works

Algorithm Workflow

SchedX constructs academic timetables by synthesizing configured teachers, subjects, sections, working days, periods, and institutional scheduling constraints into a unified, conflict-free schedule.

1Configuration
→
2Analysis
→
3Generation
→
4Validation
→
5Final Schedule
Step 1 Input Configuration
  • Teachers: Faculty profiles with assigned subject qualifications and daily limits.
  • Subjects: Course codes, assigned credit units, and weekly lecture hour requirements.
  • Sections: Student cohorts and expected class sizes.
  • Working days: Academic operating days (Monday through Sunday).
  • Periods & time slots: Operating start/end times and day-specific period limits.
  • Scheduling constraints: Laboratory room requirements and break reservations.
Step 2 Scheduling Analysis
  • Analyze teacher-subject assignments and eligibility mappings.
  • Identify available scheduling slots across active working days.
  • Check configured constraints (laboratory requirements, lunch & tea breaks).
  • Identify potential capacity bottlenecks and resource contention before placement.
Step 3 Timetable Generation
  • Assign eligible subjects and qualified teachers to suitable time slots.
  • Respect configured working days and day-specific period bounds (e.g. Mon: 5, Tue: 7, Wed: 4).
  • Attempt to avoid teacher, room, and student section scheduling conflicts.
Step 4 Validation
  • Check the generated period assignments against institutional rules.
  • Identify scheduling conflicts (double-booked faculty, occupied rooms, or student collisions).
  • Report classes that could not be scheduled with clear actionable diagnostic feedback.
Step 5 Final Timetable
  • Display the generated timetable in an interactive weekly grid view.
  • Allow users to review, filter by section/faculty/room, and edit cell assignments where supported.
Implemented Capabilities: Multi-constraint heuristic generation, teacher eligibility filtering, laboratory room enforcement, day-specific period capacities, break intervals, and 3-way collision prevention are active in SchedX. Automated multi-semester global genetic re-optimization is on the planned development roadmap.
